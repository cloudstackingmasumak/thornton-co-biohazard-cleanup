Biohazard odors originate from the decomposition of organic material. When bodily fluids, blood, or human tissue decompose, they release volatile organic compounds that create the characteristic smell associated with biohazard scenes. These compounds are extremely pungent even in tiny concentrations.

Decomposition odors vary depending on how long the body has been present. Early stages produce less distinct smells. As decomposition progresses, the odor becomes more intense and complex. Advanced decomposition produces the strongest, most persistent odors that can permeate an entire Thornton home.

Vehicle biohazard cleanup requires containment to prevent cross-contamination and protect workers. The vehicle is isolated, and areas are sealed if necessary to prevent contaminated air or material from escaping during cleanup.

Technicians wear appropriate PPE including full-body protection, gloves, respiratory protection, and shoe covers.
